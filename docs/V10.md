# SDr Scanner V10 user manual

**Version 10.1.5** · SDr Scanner runs a receive-only RTL-SDR radio on a Linux host. Use its browser controls to tune, scan, decode supported signals, view the spectrum, and record audio.

## Start listening

1. Connect the antenna and USB RTL-SDR to the Ubuntu or Debian receiver host. Follow the [install instructions](../README.md#install-on-ubuntu-or-debian).
2. Open `http://127.0.0.1:8766/` on the host, or use the receiver’s LAN name or IP address on another device. If you installed V10 alongside another scanner service, use the port assigned to V10. The V10 receiver opens at `/`.
3. Press **POWER** to start reception from this tab. Hold **POWER** for one second to return to standby and release this tab’s tuner assignment.
4. Tap the large frequency readout. Enter a frequency in MHz on the LCD keypad or with the keyboard, then press **ENT** or **Enter**. You can enter `28.176812` or the LCD format `28.176.812`. For 198 kHz, enter `0.198`. Direct frequency entry switches the receiver to **AUTO**.
5. Press **MODE** at the right end of the lower button row beneath the LCD to select a receive mode. For example, choose **DMR** to listen for DMR on the frequency you entered.
6. Turn the fixed **VOLUME** knob at the lower right to set listening level. Turn the **Squelch** dial, **D** by default, to set how strong a signal must be to open audio. In narrow FM, the receiver also checks that the signal rises above the noise floor. While listening to a single FM channel, lower SQL at least 3 dB below the estimated noise floor to hear the hiss. With its press action set to **Automatic**, press that dial to toggle **Monitor** and temporarily open squelch.

The panel accepts 100 kHz to 1.75 GHz. Coverage depends on the RTL-SDR and antenna. Below 25 MHz, the receiver uses direct sampling. Use an antenna and a dongle that support those frequencies.

### Volume and mute

Scroll or drag the fixed **VOLUME** knob, or focus it and use the arrow keys. A dial assigned to **AF Volume** adjusts the same listening level. The dedicated knob keeps its volume function permanently. The receiver stays on at zero volume.

Press the knob to mute your browser. **MUTE** appears at the upper right of the local LCD and the knob’s indicator turns amber. Press it again to restore audio. By default, mute dims the local LCD to 10% of its normal brightness. Set **MENU → SET → Display → Mute Button Dim → OFF** to mute at your usual brightness. The second LCD keeps its brightness and continues to show receiver activity.

Volume, mute, mute dimming and audio boost are local to your browser. They survive a refresh. An active tab can resume its listening session after refresh. A new tab opens in standby. Tabs listening through the same tuner share tuning and receiver settings.

**MENU** opens the main LCD screen. Its buttons are **SCOPE**, **SCAN**, **MEMORY**, **RECORD**, **HISTORY** and **SET**, with **DECODE** shown in DMR or FSK mode. The menu also shows the version. **GitHub** opens the maintainer’s profile and **User Manual** opens this guide in a new tab.

| --- | --- |
| **POWER** | Press the round switch to power on. Hold while on to return to standby. The LED ring pulses with live receive audio, including when the browser is muted, and dims when reception goes quiet. A tap while on captures a PNG if **MENU → SET → Function → Screen Capture · POWER Switch** is enabled. |
| **MODE** | Choose the receive mode. The active mode appears at the upper left of the LCD. |
| **REC** | Arm or stop MP3 recording. Amber means armed and red means recording. |
| **SCAN** / **S.SCAN** / **PRIO** | Open or stop a scan, search from the current frequency, or check a priority memory. |
| **LOCK OUT / CLEAR** | Skip the current frequency, or toggle skip on a recalled memory. The amber LED lights when the current frequency or memory is skipped. Hold to clear temporary lockouts. |
| **▲GRP** / **▼GRP** | Step through populated groups in memory mode. Open group selection while in VFO. |
| **V/M** / **MW** | Switch between VFO and memory, or open memory writing. |
| **M-CH DIAL** | Toggle the main dial between memory stepping and its assigned function. |
| **FUNCTION** | Open reception controls for the active mode. |
| **M.SCOPE** | Show or hide the scope. Use **MENU → SCOPE** to open it expanded. |
| **QUICK** | Open the configured shortcuts. |
| **EXIT** | Go back one level. On the main display it leaves the expanded scope. |
| **SPEECH / LOCK** | Tap to hear the frequency and mode spoken. Hold to lock or unlock the main tuning dial. **MENU → SET → Function → SPEECH/LOCK Switch** can swap those gestures. |

### Navigate the LCD

Tap a menu button or turn **Dial C** to highlight an item and press it to select. The other configurable dials and the main dial can also select a highlighted item. Use on-screen **▲**, **▼** and **↩** where shown, or use keyboard arrows, **Enter** and **Esc**. **EXIT** and right-click on a button or dial also go back. Right-clicking a populated favourite instead opens its clear prompt.

### Handheld layout on a phone

Mobile view follows the receive controls and navigation of the Icom IC-F4400D family: a compact light LCD, BANK −/BANK + keys, programmable P1/P2 keys beside a silver-edged directional pad, Menu/OK, Back/Home and a numeric keypad. The mobile waterfall is hidden. Desktop controls, dial assignments and the read-only `/lcd` display remain unchanged.

**BANK − / BANK +** selects the previous or next populated favourite bank and recalls its first saved channel. Bank navigation wraps and skips empty banks. Channels, presets and bookmarks remain under **MENU → Channels**; normal and preset scan controls remain under **MENU → Scan**. **L/O** sits below Menu/OK and lights amber for a skipped frequency or memory; hold it for one second to clear temporary lockouts. **S.SCAN**, below Back, starts or stops a simple frequency scan; hold it to reverse direction.

While **S.SCAN** is running, the D-pad **− / Left** sets the search direction downwards and **+ / Right** sets it upwards without restarting the scan. **Up/Down** continues to select saved channels in the current bank. Inside a menu, the D-pad continues to navigate the menu.

Tap the LED-style **REC** text at the top-right of the mobile LCD to enable or disable recording. Grey means off, amber means armed and waiting for squelch to open, and red means audio is being recorded. It uses the same recording control as the desktop REC button and menu. Menus and entry screens prevent accidental taps on the LCD control underneath.

**P1** defaults to Quick Menu and **P2** to Monitor. Change them in **MENU → Settings → Mobile Controls**, or **MENU → SET → Mobile Controls** on the desktop. Holding P1 or P2 opens its assignment directly. Assignments include None, frequency entry, recording, mute, mode, channels, bank selection, bookmarks, scan speed, lockout and other receive controls. P1/P2 are included in Settings Backup and cross-browser SYNC, separately from the dial backup. Their normal shortcuts are suspended while a menu or entry screen is open.

On the home screen, **Up/Down** steps through saved channels in the selected bank, in channel-number order, even while in VFO. It skips empty slots and wraps within that bank. An empty bank leaves tuning unchanged. The D-pad **− / Left** and **+ / Right** tunes down or up by the selected tuning step.

Mobile LCD menus are display-only text lists. In **RADIO MENU**, **Left/Right** browses categories. **Up/Down** also moves the highlight. Inside a category, **Up/Down** highlights an item, field or value. Hold either arrow to move through a long list. **MENU/OK** and the centre **OK** both select the highlighted item. **BACK** goes up one level and restores your previous selection. Hold **BACK** for one second to return home.

In a text or number picker, **Left/Right** moves the cursor and **Up/Down** highlights an entry key. Press **OK** to use that key. Hold **OK** or press **#** to accept the draft and return to the form. With a field highlighted, the number keys enter digits, **\*** erases and holding **\*** clears the field. To apply the form, highlight its save row and press **OK**, or press **#**. The LCD shows a short guide to the keys on the current screen. Compact frequency, channel and PIN entry keeps **MENU/OK**, **Left/Right** and **BACK** above the keypad.

**Channels → Tuning Step** sets how far each manual tuning press changes the frequency. **Audio / RF → Receive Filter** is available in supported analogue modes. **Scan** provides reverse direction during an active scan and hold/resume when scanning preset ranges or receiving a Nearby signal. **Scope → Desktop Scope Controls** adjusts the scope for this browser's desktop layout and its linked Second Display. Open **Scope → Open Second Display** to view the spectrum and waterfall. The handheld LCD stays compact.

On the home screen, press **0–9** to recall **CH0–CH9 in the current favourite bank immediately**, including while in VFO. On a short screen, first press **FAV 0–9** between the bank keys to show the keypad. The LCD shows the current bank. An empty favourite reports **EMPTY** and keeps the current frequency. A physical keyboard uses the same shortcuts in mobile view.

For direct frequency entry, tap the frequency display, choose **MENU → Settings → Direct Frequency Entry**, or assign Frequency Entry to P1/P2. Once that screen is open, keypad numbers enter the frequency normally: **\*** inserts the decimal point and **#** or **MENU/OK** applies it. Enter **44600625** without a decimal point to tune **446.006250 MHz**, displayed as **446.006.250**. Compact entry uses three MHz digits followed by up to six fractional digits; pad frequencies below 100 MHz with a leading zero, such as **098400000** for 98.4 MHz. A full ten-digit entry uses four MHz digits and six fractional digits. Normal decimal and grouped entries remain supported.

For channels above CH9, **\*** or **#** from the memory-mode home screen opens channel entry: **002** recalls CH2 automatically, and **#** or **MENU/OK** confirms a shorter entry. A prefix above 099 switches to frequency entry. In VFO, those home keys open frequency entry directly. Hold **\*** to delete; in channel and PIN entry, a tap deletes. PIN screens and menu editors retain their normal numeric-entry behaviour. These keypad mappings are scanner adaptations; the LCD menu keyboard remains available for text and hexadecimal setup codes.

The full keypad is visible on taller portrait screens. In shorter windows it opens for **FAV 0–9**, channel entry, frequency entry and PIN entry. **POWER** and **SYNC** are under **MENU → Settings** on mobile. In standby, **MENU → Power On** remains available, including the remote PIN flow.

The **− / VOL / +** row changes browser listening volume in 5% steps; tap its centre to mute. The phone's hardware volume buttons control media playback volume while audio is playing, independently of the radio's displayed percentage. [Apple's volume guide](https://support.apple.com/en-euro/guide/iphone/iphb71f9b54d/ios) describes that device behaviour.

Navigation reference: [Icom IC-F3400D/F4400D operating guide, sections 3 and 6](https://www.icomfrance.com/uploads/files/produit/not-IC-F3400D-SERIE-NXDN-notice-en.pdf) and [Icom UK instructions](https://icomuk.co.uk/files/icom/PDF/advancedManuals/IC-F3400D_F4400D_ENG_IM_7.pdf). Icom's programmable functions depend on the radio's programming; this scanner maps those controls to its existing receive-only features.

### Assign the dials

Turn a dial by scrolling over it or dragging it. Arrow keys also turn a focused dial. Hold **A–J** or the **main dial** for one second to open its settings, or use **MENU → SET → Dials**. Choose a turn function under **Tuning**, **Channels & Favourites**, **Audio**, **RF & Squelch**, **Screen & Scope**, or **Scan Features**. The group containing the current function shows its name and a checkmark; each function also shows the other dials using it. **Press Action**, **Sensitivity** and **None** remain on the first screen. **None** clears the turn assignment. Open **Press Action** to choose what a short press does while listening; its **Automatic** option follows the turn function, and its **None** option disables the press shortcut. Inside an LCD menu, a short press selects the highlighted item. Choices save automatically to the radio host when **SYNC** is enabled, or stay in this browser when it is off.

Open **MENU → SET → Dials Backup → Save Configuration** to download a JSON file containing the functions, press actions and sensitivities for **A–J** and the **main dial**, plus **Main Dial Auto TS** acceleration. Use **Restore Configuration** to load that file in this browser or on another device, then confirm the replacement. The whole file is checked before any dial settings change. Listening levels belong to Settings Backup.

| Turn function | Automatic short press | Other available press actions |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Frequency, fine or coarse tuning | Open frequency entry | Choose tuning step, toggle tuning lock or switch VFO and memory |
| Tuning Step | Choose the tuning step | Open frequency entry or toggle tuning lock |
| Memory Channel, Preset Channel or FAV Bank A/B | Open the corresponding channel list or preset settings | Memory groups, VFO/memory or preset scan hold, depending on the assignment |
| AF Volume or Squelch | Toggle mute or Monitor, respectively | Open the assigned setting or Audio / RF Settings |
| RF gain, RF Gain · dB, boost, treble, bass or beep level | Open the assigned setting | Audio / RF Settings and reset where offered |
| Scope Pan, Span or Reference | Recenter on the tuned frequency, open Span, or edit the reference level | Scope hold, fixed edges, full capture or marker, depending on the assignment |
| Scan Direction | Reverse a running scan or open the scan menu | Start or stop a scan, or hold a preset scan |
| Scan Speed, Pause/Resume Time or Priority Interval | Open the assigned setting | Scan settings, scan shortcuts or Priority Watch, depending on the assignment |

Choose **RF Gain · dB** to step through the input gain values supported by the connected SDR. Press the dial to see the list, or hover over either RF gain dial to see the gain currently applied. RF gain controls the signal entering the receiver. **AF Volume** and **Audio Boost** control listening level. Both RF gain controls show **Unavailable** during direct sampling, which bypasses the tuner below 25 MHz in this receiver. Dial assignments and saved tuner gain are kept for returning to a supported frequency. In AM, AGC maintains audio loudness as the RF level changes; use the spectrum or signal meter to judge input gain changes.

The automatic choices follow the contextual push-dial controls in the [Icom IC-R8600 manual](https://www.icomeurope.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/IC-R8600_ENG_IM_6.pdf), the [Yaesu FTDX10 manual](https://www.yaesu.com/Files/4CB893D7-1018-01AF-FA97E9E9AD48B50C/FTDX10_OM_ENG_EH076H202_2307A-LS-1.pdf) and [Uniden’s SDS200 waterfall guide](https://www.uniden.info/download/ompdf/SDS200Waterfallom.pdf#page=3).

Assignments include fixed 10 Hz or 10 kHz tuning, tuning step, memory channel, preset channel, favourite bank selection, audio and RF controls, scope pan, span and reference, scan direction and speed, pause and resume time, and priority interval. **Normal** sensitivity uses the usual rate, **High** doubles it, and **Very High** quadruples it.

Assign **Preset Channel** to step through the current preset zone. During a scan, turning the dial holds the channel you step to and respects scan lockouts. Press the scope’s **HOLD** key to resume the scan. When browsing recalled favourites, the dial visits saved channels marked to be skipped during scanning. Memories in Banks 0 and 1 can be browsed together. After recalling a memory from Banks 2–99, the dial browses that bank.

Select **TS** by the meter to choose the main tuning step. Hover over a frequency digit and scroll to change its place value. For example, scrolling up over the **2** in **446.006250 MHz** adds 100 Hz. Double-click the frequency to copy it with the unit. Tap the clock to view the host’s local time and UTC.

## Save and recall channels

- **FAV A** and **FAV B** each have ten direct keys and initially show Banks 0 and 1. Assign **FAV BANK A (Left)** or **FAV BANK B (Right)** to a dial to choose which memory group from 0 to 99 that panel shows. Each selection is saved in the browser. Tap **CH0–CH9** to recall a channel from the displayed group. Hold a key for one second to store the current tune there. Amber marks a saved channel and green marks the current channel. To clear a saved key, right-click it and right-click the same key again to confirm.
- Open **MENU → SET → Favourite Banks** and choose a range to name any bank from **00** to **99**. For quicker access to the two banks currently on the panel, select the **FAV A** or **FAV B** shortcut at the top. Press **SET** to save, or save an empty name to restore the numbered heading. The name follows the numbered bank and is included in settings backups.
- Tap **MW → Select the Channel and Write** to enter the group, slot, frequency, mode and optional name. Enter the relevant tone or digital filters, then press **MW** in the LCD editor. If the slot is occupied, confirm replacement. Hold the front-panel **MW** to save the current tune in the first free slot of the selected group when available.
- **MENU → MEMORY** opens the group browser. Use **GROUP** to choose a group or **CH-INP** to recall a numbered channel. Open a memory to edit its name and scan tag, set or clear its skip flag, or delete it.
- **V/M** toggles VFO and memory. In memory mode, hold it to move the current tune into VFO. Turn a dial assigned to **Memory Channel**, or enable **M-CH DIAL** and then turn the main dial to step through memories.
- **FAV** in the preset grid scans Banks 0 and 1. To scan other groups, choose **MEMO** under **SCAN**. Group A is reserved for Auto Memory Write scan hits.

## Scan zones and search

### Preset zones

Tap a **PRESETS** key to start scanning its ranges or channels. Click or Ctrl-click other keys to add them. The lit selections and the LCD’s **PRESETS** row show all active zones. **MULTI** shows the zone currently being checked. The scan rotates through short batches from each zone and pauses when it finds a signal. Click an active key to remove it. Click the last one or press **SCAN** to stop.

| Preset | Coverage |
| --- | --- |
| **FAV** | Saved memories in Banks 0 and 1 |
| **CB**, **PMR** | The UK CB channel allocations and PMR446 frequencies, with separate entries for supported modes |
| **BUSINESS**, **AMATEUR** | Separate UK band ranges with band-specific tuning steps and receive modes |
| **FM RADIO** | Broadcast FM from 87.5 to 107.9 MHz on a 100 kHz grid |
| **RAIL**, **EVENTS**, **UTILITIES** | Scan windows based on registered assignments |
| **AIR**, **MARINE** | Civil AM airband and marine VHF and onboard UHF voice ranges |
| **CUSTOM 1–12** | Your named frequency ranges in AUTO mode |
| **NEARBY** | Strong-signal watch across supported voice bands while you continue listening |

If **FM RADIO** is the only selected preset and you are already inside that band, the scan starts at the next grid frequency in the current direction and wraps at the band edge. Turn a dial assigned to **Scan Direction** to continue past a station.

Hold a preset key or open **MENU → SET → Presets** to edit its scan ranges. Choose a range or **Add Range** and set its start, end, step and receive mode. **Restore Default Ranges** returns that preset to its factory limits. Open **Stored Channels** to recall, edit or delete individual entries. **CB** and **PMR** open their editable channel lists directly. A scan across overlapping zones can check the same frequency and mode more than once.

The amateur default uses separate ranges for each supported UK band, including eleven 60 m windows and 23 cm coverage. The band limits come from the RSGB reference data and the scanner uses band-dependent grid spacing. [RSGB Full reference data](https://rsgb.services/public/exams/exam-forms/260218_EX309_Full_2026_v1.pdf) · [RSGB operating band plans](https://rsgb.org/main/operating/band-plans/)

Rail, events and utilities use receive windows around registered assignments. Events also includes the Channel 21 portable and base ranges. [Ofcom Wireless Telegraphy Register](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/spectrum/frequencies/wtr) · [Ofcom Channel 21](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/spectrum/radio-equipment/channel-21)

Configure **Custom 1–12** from their keys or **MENU → SET → Presets**. Enter the name and lower and upper frequencies. **Scan Step → Auto** uses 8.33 kHz for a range wholly inside civil airband, 100 kHz for a range wholly inside broadcast FM, 1 kHz below 30 MHz, and 6.25 kHz for other ranges. Choose an explicit step if the channels use a different grid. Custom ranges can run alongside other zones.

### Nearby, hold and scan speed

Tap **NEARBY** to start or stop watching for strong signals while tuned or during a preset scan. It checks the live capture around the tuned channel and sweeps other voice bands, revisiting PMR446 frequently. After a detection, it compares eligible signals for up to 200 ms and tunes to the strongest one it measured. An unconfirmed hit has a one-second retry delay. The sweep line moves across the scope as it searches. With only Nearby active, **LISTENING** can appear on the readout and waterfall. On a hit, the panel shows **NEARBY RX** and uses AUTO. It returns to your previous tuning seven seconds after the reception ends. A reply restarts the delay. Press the scope’s **HOLD** to stay on a hit, then press it again to resume. Hold the **NEARBY** key to see and tune previous hits.

A single tuner can briefly interrupt audio to inspect signals outside its current RF window. A short transmission can end before the tuner gets to its window. An available second tuner can run Nearby checks while the first keeps receiving.

Run `python3 tools/nearby_check.py` from the checkout to test Nearby with simulated signals. For a receive-only comparison of the area before and during a handheld transmission, run `python3 tools/nearby_check.py --live` on the SDR host with a free tuner. Add `--band handheld --seconds 20` to check 136–174 MHz and 400–480 MHz when the channel is unknown. The test reports candidate frequencies and signal levels.

While scanning presets, press **HOLD** on scope page **<1>** to pause or resume. Selecting a point on the spectrum or waterfall also pauses the preset scan to inspect that frequency. Moving away from a digital preset can switch inspection to AUTO. Resuming returns to the preset’s stored mode. **HOLD** or **SCAN HOLD** indicates an engaged hold. On page **<2>**, **SPEED** sets the preset scan rate. A preset scan stays on an open signal, then waits for the configured **Resume Timer** after it closes.

Open **QUICK → Scan Speed** to choose the target rate and time spent checking each quiet frequency: **1 /s · 1 s** through **30 /s · 33 ms**. For example, **8 /s · 125 ms** checks about eight quiet frequencies per second before hardware and mode acquisition limits. Assign this control to any dial under **MENU → SET → Dials → [dial] → Scan Features → Scan Speed**. **QUICK → Scan Pause Time**, also under the dial's **Scan Features**, limits how long ordinary scans pause after finding an active signal; **HOLD** has no time limit. Preset scans keep following an active signal until it ends, then use **Resume Timer**. Both shortcuts are enabled initially and can be hidden under **MENU → SET → Quick Menu**.

Turn on **MENU → SCAN → SET → Scan Follows Scope** to link scan rate to the selected spectrum span. It applies to S.SCAN, programmed ranges, presets and memory scans. Spans up to 100 kHz use the base **Scan Speed**. Wider spans increase the target rate up to twice the base speed at 1.8 MHz, capped at 30 checks per second. Mode acquisition times can reduce the actual rate. Tuning steps, open-signal holds and pause or resume timers keep their existing behaviour. The receiver setting defaults to **OFF**. With it enabled, changing the selected span while scanning updates that scan's rate.

### Scan types, priority and lockouts

| Control | Operation |
| --- | --- |
| **SCAN → PROG** | Search within stored scan edges or a configured frequency range. |
| **SCAN → ΔF** | Search within a span around a centre frequency. |
| **SCAN → AUTO** | Search a range and save accepted hits in memory Group A. |
| **SCAN → MEMO** | Scan stored memories, optionally limited to a group. |
| **SCAN → SEL** | Scan memories marked with a select tag. |
| **SCAN → MODE** | Scan memories with a specified mode. |
| **S.SCAN** | Start searching up from the current frequency with the current tuning step. Hold to start down. While running, turn the main dial or hold **S.SCAN** to change direction. Tap to stop. |
| **PRIO** | Check the selected memory at the configured interval. Hold during priority watch to stop the watch and recall that memory. |
| **LOCK OUT / CLEAR** | Exclude a frequency while scanning or tuning manually. With scanning stopped on a memory, toggle its saved skip flag. Hold to clear temporary lockouts. |

Memory, select-tag and mode scans need at least two eligible memories. **SCAN** stops the running scan. Hold it while idle to reuse the last scan. Open **MENU → SCAN → SET** for pause, resume, speed, temporary skip and main-dial behaviour. **FINE** uses 10 Hz steps during a programmed or delta-frequency scan. **RECALL** returns to the previous frequency. Timed **SKIP** expires after the configured interval. Temporary frequency lockouts survive a scan restart and clear when the receiver returns to standby.

## Receive modes and decoding

| Mode | Use |
| --- | --- |
| **AUTO** | Select WFM in the broadcast FM band and AM in civil airband or the configured long- and medium-wave broadcast bands. Elsewhere it starts with narrow FM and can detect AM or confirmed clear DMR, P25 Phase I and NXDN-VN voice. |
| **FM** / **WFM** | Narrow FM voice and wide FM broadcasting respectively. |
| **AM** / **S-AM(D/U/L)** | AM voice or broadcast reception, or synchronous AM using both, upper or lower sidebands. |
| **USB** / **LSB** | Single-sideband signals. |
| **CW** / **CW-R** | Morse code with normal or reverse beat-note direction. |
| **FSK** / **FSK-R** | RTTY using normal or reversed mark and space. **MENU → DECODE** shows the ITA2 text and decode settings. |
| **DMR**, **D-STAR**, **P25**, **dPMR**, **NXDN-VN** | Supported conventional digital voice modes. Use **MODE** to select one directly. P25 is Phase I and NXDN-VN is very narrowband. Reception depends on decoder, hardware and signal quality. |

With DSD-neo installed, **AUTO** checks a strong, centred narrowband signal for a valid clear voice call. The LCD shows the detected mode next to **AUTO** and returns to analogue reception after the signal ends. NXDN detection requires confirmation at the NXDN48 symbol rate. Select the mode directly if brief transmissions end before AUTO confirms them.

Tap **FIL** to choose a filter or set bandwidth where supported. **FUNCTION** shows mode-specific controls such as AGC, notch, noise blanker, noise reduction, tone or digital squelch. **MENU → SET → Tone Control** adjusts the audio filters and EQ exposed for the selected mode. **Audio Boost** adds up to 24 dB to live audio in this browser.

For steady hiss on AM, narrow FM or SSB voice, open **FUNCTION → NR**, turn **Noise Reduction** on and start at **LEVEL 7**. If weak syllables sound muffled, lower the level. The Linux host filters the audio for live listening, replay and new recordings. NR is saved for the selected receive mode. Level **0** bypasses the filter.

For FM tones, open **FUNCTION → TONE → T-SCAN** to detect CTCSS or DCS. Recall an FM memory and use **T-SQL / DTCS** to enter its matching tone or code. A CTCSS entry looks like `123.0` and a DCS entry like `D023N`. For P25 or NXDN, **FUNCTION → D.SQL** can filter by NAC or RAN.

To view broadcast FM station data, select **WFM** and turn on **MENU → SET → Display → RDS**. The station name and RadioText appear when the signal carries decodable RDS.

### DMR voice, SMS and Tier III

1. Select **MODE → DMR** and tune to a receive frequency. Open **MENU → DECODE → Conventional → Program Channel** to save it. You can leave **Colour Code**, **Time Slot** and **Talkgroup** blank or fill them in to filter the memory. **D.SQL** separately lets you select any, group or individual calls.
2. Read supported received SMS under **MENU → DECODE → DMR SMS** or **MENU → HISTORY → DMR SMS**. Open a message for its sender, destination, frequency and text. Use **Delete** and confirm to remove the message.
3. For a Tier III site, open **MENU → DECODE → Tier III → Site Set**. Enter the control channel in MHz and a comma-separated LCN-to-frequency map. Include the control frequency in the map. For example, use the format `101=460.125, 102=460.225`, substituting the actual site values. Add any talkgroup names, hold, block or allow-list rules, then select **SET**.
4. Select **START** to follow the configured site. A single tuner moves from the control channel to a mapped traffic channel for supported clear group voice and back to control afterwards. The screen shows the control and traffic channels when known. Stop with **STOP** or **SCAN**.

The app is receive-only and plays clear digital calls. It uses DSD-neo on the receiver host. Run the complete [installer](../README.md#run-the-full-installer) to set up the bundled decoder.

## Spectrum, waterfall and second display

- **<1>** and **<2>** cycle between the scope control pages. **SPAN** and dials assigned to **Scope Span** let you zoom from 12.5 kHz out to the full live capture, up to 1.8 MHz. **Full capture** opens **CAPTURE**, which shows the whole SDR window and can put the tuned frequency off-centre. **CENT/FIX** returns to **CENTER** from CAPTURE, PAN or FIX. In CENTER, the green line stays at the midpoint. The span fits symmetrically where possible, and any area outside the captured data is blank. Press **CENT/FIX** again to enter **FIX**, which keeps both frequency edges steady as you tune. **FIX <<** or **FIX >>** means the tuned frequency is outside those edges. Use **EDGE** to edit the limits or use the current capture.
- Turn **F · Scope Pan**, to the left of the main dial, to move the spectrum and waterfall within the SDR’s live capture while the radio stays on the tuned frequency. Each turn moves by 10% of the visible width at normal sensitivity. Use **SPAN** to zoom in before panning when the whole capture is visible. Press **F** to return to the centred view. The panned view is remembered in the browser.
- **HOLD** freezes or resumes the scope during ordinary tuning. In CENTER, the green line marks the midpoint of the frozen scope until the view is recentred or released. In FIX, the tuned-frequency marker still follows live tuning. Hold **HOLD** to clear the stored peak trace. During a preset scan or a Nearby hit, the same button controls the receiver hold.
- **REF** adjusts the reference level. **SPEED** cycles scope update speed unless a preset scan is active. **MARKER** toggles the peak marker. Tap **EXPD/SET** to switch between the regular display and a view with the spectrum, waterfall, and scope controls. Hold it to open scope settings, including Max Hold, averaging, waterfall visibility and waterfall speed.
- **QUICK → Snap to Carrier** is on by default. Tap a signal in the live spectrum or recent waterfall rows to tune to its estimated carrier. A tap still tunes to the indicated frequency when the signal is ambiguous, the row is old, or the receive mode uses a suppressed carrier. Direct frequency entry and dial tuning use their usual steps.
- Hover over the plot for the frequency at the pointer. Tap or click to tune to it. A wheel notch over the plot adjusts tuning by 1% of the displayed span. In **CAPTURE**, every waterfall row fills the plot using the frequency range captured at that time. Hover and click follow the selected row’s frequencies. **FIX** keeps older rows aligned to the fixed frequency axis and leaves uncaptured areas blank.
- Saved frequencies have amber highlights behind the spectrum trace. They widen as you zoom in with **Scope Span**, making saved frequencies easier to see. Hover anywhere within a highlight to see the saved name, bank and channel. The highlights include saved channels from every memory bank within the visible frequency range.
- The dashed **SQL** line shows the receiver’s squelch threshold in dBFS. The white diamond on the tuned frequency shows the channel power compared with that threshold. The blue trace shows individual FFT bins, so a signal spread across several bins can open squelch even when its highest bin is below the line. The bar shows signal strength above the receiver noise floor. **QUICK → Meter Type** switches the number beside it between dBFS and relative strength. The SQL line disappears when receiver squelch is fully open.

Open `/lcd` on a phone, computer or HDMI monitor for a read-only view of the active receiver. It opens without a PIN and shows the current frequency, selected zones including custom names, and the spectrum and waterfall, with no audio or tuner controls. **QUICK → Second Display** opens the same address and, in the same browser, can link it to the selected control tab and show its menus. If the control tab uses local audio mute, the second LCD continues to show receiver activity at normal brightness.

## Recordings, history and replay

1. To choose where clips go, open **MENU → RECORD → Recorder Set → REC FOLDER**. Enter a writable directory on the Linux receiver host and press **SET**.
2. Press **REC** beside **LOCK OUT** to arm it. The receiver starts a clip when eligible audio opens squelch. Press **REC** again to disarm. Completed clips of at least two seconds are saved as MP3 files.
3. Open **MENU → RECORD → Play Files** and select the latest file. **STOP** returns to live audio. When history audio is available, **Instant Replay** plays the most recent captured activity. It can also be added to **QUICK**.

Recordings use the receiver host’s local time and six decimal places for the frequency. A clip from the named memory **CH2 – London Info** is saved as `CH2 - London Info/14:09:15 - 15.11.2026- 135.375000 MHz.mp3` under the chosen directory. An unnamed memory uses its bank number, such as `CH9 - Bank 1`. When tuning manually, the file is saved directly in the chosen directory.

**QUICK → Search Recordings** or **MENU → RECORD → Search Recordings** filters completed MP3s by channel, folder or name, date, and exact frequency in MHz. Leave filters blank to browse the latest clips. Choose a result to play it and use **STOP** to return to live audio.

**QUICK → RF Replay** freezes up to the last 10 seconds of raw samples from the current SDR capture. Change **Replay Frequency** and **Replay Mode** to listen anywhere within the displayed range in a supported analogue mode. Play the same capture again at another frequency without moving the live tuner. **Capture Latest RF** takes a fresh snapshot. Captures expire after three minutes and are held in server memory. A hardware retune or interruption can shorten the available window.

Open **MENU → HISTORY → RX History** for recent activity. Choose an entry to see capture duration, station or memory name, received or detected mode, tone or code, peak signal, SNR and estimated carrier offset when available. Digital entries can also show radio IDs, talkgroups and other decoded information. **PLAY** plays captured audio. **REC** saves an eligible captured clip in the recording directory. Logging starts when V10 powers on. **Pause Log** and **Resume Log** control new entries. History is held in memory. Save clips you want to keep before restarting the scanner service.

## Bookmarks and UK repeaters

- Choose **QUICK → Bookmarks → Bookmark Tuned Frequency** to save the current frequency and receive mode with a name. Open **Personal Bookmarks** to recall, rename or delete an entry. Each browser keeps its own bookmarks.
- Choose **QUICK → Bookmarks → UK Repeaters** and select a band from 10 m to 23 cm. Browse its full list using a dial, the mouse wheel, or a swipe. **Filter** stays above the list and searches by callsign, town, locator or receive mode. Select a result to see its published status, then choose a mode to tune to its output or save it as a personal bookmark. The receiver host fetches the directory from the [RSGB ETCC UK Repeater API](https://ukrepeater.net/csvfiles.html) and caches each band for up to a day.
- Under **UK Repeaters → My Locator**, enter your 4, 6 or 8 character Maidenhead locator and select **SET**. Open **All Bands** or an individual band to see the closest repeaters first. Distances in miles are approximate and use the centres of the published locator squares. Entries missing a valid locator appear last. Your browser remembers your locator. Leave it blank to return to callsign order.
- When tuned to a listed output, the callsign appears above the frequency. A **+** count indicates other possible matches on that output. Hover over the callsign for the published details and the other callsigns. Selecting a repeater from the directory or a saved repeater bookmark gives that callsign priority. Otherwise your locator puts nearby matches first.

Use **Save Setting** or **Load Setting** from the scanner host or an [SSH tunnel to the host](../README.md#ssh-tunnel). The same access is required for receiver resets and for changing the recording folder. The settings file contains the recording folder's path; MP3 recordings, RF captures, receive history, installed software and active browser sessions are separate. Restoring on another host requires a valid writable recording folder there. ## Connect from another device

### Keep the same setup in every browser

The square **SYNC** toggle with its lower light bar shares the compact recessed panel beside the round **POWER** button and is enabled by default. It saves dial functions, press actions, sensitivities, acceleration, audio preferences, display and waterfall colours, scope settings, favourite bank choices, bookmarks, repeater location, Quick Menu choices and scan preferences on the radio host. The last intentionally selected frequency, mode, squelch and history preference are remembered for powering on. Receiver programming and memories already live on that host.

After an update, refresh the browser containing your preferred setup first so it can create the initial saved profile. Open the same scanner address on your work PC, tablet or phone to load it. Later changes save automatically and appear in other synced browsers. The light bar is cyan when saved, amber while loading or saving, and coral when the host cannot be reached. Desktop captions also show ON/OFF for power and SAVED, LOADING, SAVING, RETRY or LOCAL for sync. Local changes are retained for retrying.

Turn **SYNC** off for a browser that needs its own setup. Turning it back on saves that browser's current setup as the shared one. Loading a profile does not retune an active receiver or start scanning or recording. Normal PIN authentication still applies on remote connections; sessions and login credentials stay separate. **Settings Backup** and **Dials Backup** remain available, and restoring their preferences also updates the shared setup while SYNC is on.

A trusted, direct LAN connection or known Tailscale peer can open the scanner without entering a PIN. See the [private network access instructions](../README.md#access-from-another-computer) for supported hostnames, routed LANs and port configuration. Each active tuner can receive one frequency at a time. Additional available dongles let the host assign independent tuners to other listening tabs.

### Set a remote PIN

1. Open the panel on the scanner host or through an [SSH tunnel](../README.md#ssh-tunnel) and choose **MENU → SET → PIN**. You can set it while listening, scanning or in standby.
2. Enter a numeric PIN of your chosen length and select **ENT** or press **Enter**.
3. Enter the same PIN again to save. Leading zeroes are preserved.

For PIN setup from a trusted LAN or Tailscale browser, first issue a one-time setup code on the scanner host. Use the port the scanner listens on.

```bash
curl --fail --silent --show-error -X POST \
  -H 'Origin: http://127.0.0.1:8766' \
  http://127.0.0.1:8766/api/access/setup
```

Enter the code under **MENU → SET → PIN** within five minutes, then enter and confirm the new PIN.

On a connection that requires remote authentication, press **POWER** and enter the PIN to unlock reception. PIN access gives you receiver controls. Direct trusted clients can open the read-only `/lcd` separately. The host owner can enable remote viewing of that page with `--public-display`. [Remote domain and forwarded-IP access](../README.md#use-a-domain-or-forwarded-ip-address)

## Troubleshooting

| Symptom | What to check |
| --- | --- |
| **SDR UNAVAILABLE** | Confirm the dongle is attached to the Linux host. Close other software using that USB device. Return listening tabs to standby before testing with `rtl_test`. |
| The spectrum moves but audio is silent | Press a control to enable browser audio. Check **MUTE**, local **VOLUME**, receiver mode and squelch. Lower the SQL threshold or use **Monitor** to check a weak signal. |
| A scan stops on noise or misses signals | Check the antenna, mode and squelch. Check the preset's start, end and channel grid. Allow time for a full pass across wide ranges. |
| A preset scan stays on one frequency | Check the LCD for **HOLD** or **PAUSE**. Press scope **HOLD** to resume a manual hold. For a signal holding the scan open, turn the assigned **Scan Direction** dial to continue. |
| WFM audio works but RDS is blank | Enable **MENU → SET → Display → RDS**, then give the station time to decode. Check reception and whether the station transmits RDS. |
| Digital decoder reports an error | Run the complete installer on the receiver host, verify your mode and channel settings, and check the signal and service journal. |
| No MP3 appears | Check the receiver host’s configured folder and permissions. Arm **REC** and let an eligible signal finish after at least two seconds. |
| The second LCD is in standby | Power on a control panel with an available tuner. To mirror that tab’s menus as well, use **QUICK → Second Display** in the same browser. |

See the [Icom parity audit](../reports/SDr%20Scanner%20V10%20parity.md) for the software coverage and remaining hardware checks, and the [dial and Quick Menu reference](../reports/Receiver%20dial%20assignments.md) for design references.
